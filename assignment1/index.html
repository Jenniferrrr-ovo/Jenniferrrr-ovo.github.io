Interactive Experience Research Project

Website: Frank Gehry – Getty

Author: s4173160@student.rmit.edu.au

1. What was the first thing you paid attention to?

My first impression that view this website is that it has a strong contrast between the light yellow background and black font

2. Spend two minutes with the experience and create a list of each of your discrete actions.

Over two minutes, I moved through most of the website by scrolling through its main sections, including the introduction, Ship in the Box, Sails of Steel, Bouquet of Sound, A Place to Gather, and the Index.

  • Opened the website and observed the landing page.
  • Scrolled down through the introduction.
  • Paused to watch the video.
  • Continued scrolling into “Ship in the Box.”
  • Looked closely at the images and accompanying text.
  • Scrolled further into “Sails of Steel.”
  • Paused again to read and observe the visual transitions.
  • Continued into “Bouquet of Sound.”
  • Scrolled into “A Place to Gather.”
  • Finished by reaching the Index section.

3.What part of the experience did you spend the most time engaging with?

The most distinctive feature of this website is that it incorporates introduction videos as well as 3D models of the buildings. Therefore, I spent most of my time watching the videos.

4.What was the most common action in your two minutes interaction with the experience?

The most common action is to scroll downwards. The entire website experience is based on vertical movement, and scrolling is the primary means of advancing the storyline.

5.What is your impression of the intended primary goal of the interactive experience?

The main purpose of this website is to explore the construction process of Walt Disney Concert Hall, which was designed by Frank Gehry. And focus on the creativity and experimentation behind the building.

6.How does the interactive experience communicate this primary goal?

The experience communicates this goal through a combination of images, short text sections, and sequential storytelling. Instead of presenting all information on one page. It reveals the design process step by step, which helps the user understand the progression of ideas over time.

7.What is your impression of how the experience should be interacted with over time? (For how long and how many different times)

This website is designed to be interacted with in a one-time, continuous session. It encourages users to spend several minutes scrolling through the content from top to bottom, just like viewing a complete exhibition. While it can review the website, it does not suggest repeated viewing, as the website presents a complete narrative.

8.How does the interactive experience communicate how it should be interacted with over time?

The experience communicates through linear scrolling, presenting the content continuously. There aren’t clear navigation options for selection; therefore, it encourages users to read from beginning to end.

9.What other media forms (digital or otherwise) does the experience reference?

This experience is inspired by museum exhibitions or documentary narratives. It makes the user feel like immersed in a real gallery.

10.What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should act when engaging with your research experience?

These references suggest that the user should move through the experience slowly and deliberately, pausing at each section to observe the visuals and read the text carefully rather than quickly scrolling past the content.

11.What does this reference/s communicate to you about how you should feel when engaging with your research experience?

These references suggest that users should feel curious and immersed while engaging with the experience. It creates a calm and reflective atmosphere similar to a museum exhibition.

12.What is the most frustrating part of the interaction to you, and what makes that part frustrating?

The most frustrating part of the interaction for me is the use of large typography, which at times overwhelms the screen and makes it difficult to read comfortably. While the oversized text creates a strong visual impact, it also mess the flow of reading. Therefore, I need to adjust my focus and scrolling to fully understand the content. This tension between visual design and readability makes the experience slightly challenging, especially during longer interactions.

13.What is the most satisfying part of the interaction to you and what makes that part satisfying?

The most satisfying part of the interaction for me is the seamless transition between images driven by scroll-based interaction. The visuals shift smoothly, creating a strong sense of visual continuity and immersive experience. This makes the experience feel cinematic.
